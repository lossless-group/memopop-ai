# Stage Lens — Reading the 5 O's by Round

The rubric in `12ps-5os-rubric.yaml` defines what each O and P means and how the
1–5 scale reads. Its text is deliberately stage-agnostic. This overlay adjusts *the
bar* — what a given score should require — depending on the round the company is
raising. Apply it on top of the rubric; never replace the rubric's definitions.

**How to use this file:** After establishing the company's stage, hold the matching
stage column in mind as you form each O-level score (default mode) or each P-level
score that rolls into an O (precision mode). When a score is materially affected by
the stage lens, say so in that item's rationale ("scored against seed expectations").

The core principle: **earlier stages are scored on evidence of insight and potential;
later stages are scored on evidence of proof.** A pre-seed Opportunity 5 looks nothing
like a Series A Opportunity 5, and conflating them is the most common scoring error.

---

## Stage definitions

- **Pre-seed** — Idea to early prototype. Little or no revenue. Raising to find PMF. Often pre-product or private beta.
- **Seed** — Product in market. Early revenue or strong usage signal. Raising to establish repeatable go-to-market and reach PMF.
- **Series A** — Demonstrated PMF. Meaningful, growing revenue. Raising to scale a proven engine.

For unusual deals (e.g., deep-tech pre-revenue raising an A on technical milestones),
note the mismatch and score against the stage whose *expectations* best fit the raise,
not the label.

---

## How the bar shifts, by O

### Origins (Persona, Pain, Proposition)
Most stage-stable of the five, but the evidence standard rises.
- **Pre-seed** — Deep qualitative understanding earns a high score even without scaled research; founder insight into persona and pain is the underwriting.
- **Seed** — Expect persona/pain validated by real early-customer behavior, not just interviews.
- **Series A** — Expect the proposition validated by conversion and retention, not just clarity.

### Opening (Problem, Possibility, Positioning)
Market size and trend are stage-agnostic; the *rigor* and *evidence of moat* rise.
- **Pre-seed** — Credible top-down market logic plus a defensible SAM story is enough; the moat can be *intended* and reasoned.
- **Seed** — Expect bottom-up SOM grounded in early sales/usage data; moat should be starting to show (early retention, switching costs).
- **Series A** — Expect market penetration evidence and a moat that is demonstrably forming; hand-wavy market math caps Opening at 3.

### Organization (People, Process, Product)
- **Pre-seed** — Founder-market fit carries most of this O; minimal process is fine; a rough product that nails the core value scores well. Score potential and technical judgment.
- **Seed** — Expect a forming growth engine, shipping cadence, and a product users demonstrably value.
- **Series A** — Expect operational maturity, a proven growth engine, and product polish with evidence of love (retention, NPS, advocacy).

### Offering (synthesis of Proposition + Positioning + Product)
A coherence read across three P's from other O's — how well the promise, the
competitive frame, and the built thing line up.
- **Pre-seed** — Coherence of *intent* is enough: does the promise, the positioning, and the early product point the same direction?
- **Seed** — Expect coherence validated by early market response.
- **Series A** — Expect the offering to cohere *in market* — customers buy the positioning, the product delivers the proposition, and it compounds.

### Opportunity (Potential, Progress, Plan)
The most stage-dependent O. This is where "insight vs. proof" bites hardest.
- **Pre-seed** — Score on the *shape* of the opportunity and the believability of the "what would have to be true" logic. Progress = demand signal (design partners, LOIs, waitlist conversion, prototype pull); revenue is a bonus. Plan = a credible path to *seed* milestones with ~18–24mo runway. A 5 is exceptional signal for pre-seed, not exceptional revenue.
- **Seed** — Score on early growth rates, cohort behavior, and initial recurring revenue. Progress = early PMF evidence and a repeatable (if small) channel. Plan = a credible path to *Series A* metrics with the numbers named. A 5 needs real (if small) numbers trending well.
- **Series A** — Score on demonstrated compounding. Progress = proven, growing revenue with healthy retention and unit economics; prototype/LOI-stage evidence caps at 2 here. Plan = a path to efficient scale with disciplined capital allocation. A 5 needs top-decile-for-Series-A metrics.

---

## Relative importance of the O's by stage

The framework does not hardcode O-weights (those are per-partner, summing to 5). But
the *typical* emphasis shifts by round, and the written summary should reflect it —
via the narrative, not by silently overriding a partner's chosen weights:

- **Pre-seed** — Origins and the People portion of Organization carry the thesis. You underwrite insight and founder, because proof doesn't exist yet.
- **Seed** — Opening (market) and early Opportunity (Progress/Potential) come to the fore as PMF evidence starts to matter.
- **Series A** — Opportunity dominates; the question is whether a proven engine will scale.

If a partner's chosen weights diverge sharply from the stage-typical emphasis, that is
itself interesting and worth noting — it may encode a real thesis ("I weight People
heavily even at Series A") worth surfacing to the partnership.
